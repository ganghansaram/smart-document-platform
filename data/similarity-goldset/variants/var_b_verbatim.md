# Environmental Qualification Test Procedure for Aircraft Equipment

<!-- Page 1 -->

## 1. PURPOSE

This document defines the environmental qualification test methodology applicable
to airborne equipment intended for fixed wing and rotary wing platforms operating
within the defense aviation envelope. The procedures herein establish the minimum
qualification baseline that suppliers must demonstrate prior to delivery acceptance.
Deviation from this baseline requires written approval from the responsible engineering
authority.

## 2. PROGRAM TAILORING

The applicability matrix in Table 2-1 specifies which test sequences must be performed
for each equipment category. Tailoring decisions shall consider the operational
profile, mission criticality, and prior qualification heritage of the candidate
equipment. The supplier shall submit a tailoring rationale memorandum to the
procuring authority within 14 days of contract award. Reuse of legacy qualification
data is permitted when the platform exposure profile is bounded by the prior test.

## 3. CITED STANDARDS

The following publications are invoked to the extent indicated in subsequent sections.
Where conflicts arise between this document and any cited reference, this document
shall govern. MIL-STD-810H establishes laboratory test methods. RTCA DO-160G provides
commercial transport coverage. SAE AS6171 addresses counterfeit avoidance during
test sample preparation. ISO 16750 covers ground vehicle interfaces.

## 4. LABORATORY ENVIRONMENT

### 4.1 Climatic Chamber

Temperature, humidity, and altitude testing shall be conducted in a chamber capable
of maintaining setpoints within plus or minus 2 degrees Celsius and 5 percent
relative humidity. The chamber working volume shall provide at least 150 millimeters
of clearance around the test article on all sides. Chamber recovery time after door
opening shall not exceed 10 minutes for nominal setpoints.

### 4.2 Vibration Apparatus

Mechanical vibration shall be applied using an electrodynamic shaker capable of
producing the spectra defined in Section 7. The fixture shall provide a measured
transmissibility ratio between 0.9 and 1.1 across the test bandwidth. Acceleration
control shall be performed using triaxial accelerometers bonded directly to the
test article mounting interface.

## 5. THERMAL CYCLING

### 5.1 Profile Definition

The thermal profile consists of 12 successive cycles between negative 40 and plus
71 degrees Celsius with a dwell time of 60 minutes at each extreme. Transition
rates shall not exceed 5 degrees Celsius per minute unless the equipment design
explicitly accommodates faster excursions. Power shall be applied during the final
two cycles to verify functional performance under thermal stress.

### 5.2 Pass Criteria

The equipment shall remain within specification limits during the powered cycles
and shall recover full functionality within 5 minutes of returning to ambient
conditions. Visible damage including cracking, warping, or seal degradation
constitutes a test failure regardless of functional performance. The supplier shall
document any anomalous indications observed during the cycling sequence.

## 6. HUMIDITY EXPOSURE

The humidity test consists of 10 consecutive 24 hour cycles in which the chamber
relative humidity is maintained above 95 percent with temperatures cycling between
30 and 60 degrees Celsius. Condensation on the equipment exterior is permitted
but internal moisture intrusion shall result in test failure. Functional checks
shall be performed at the conclusion of each cycle and at the end of the exposure
sequence.

## 7. RANDOM VIBRATION

### 7.1 Spectrum Selection

The random vibration spectrum shall be selected from the categories defined in
Table 7-1 based on the platform installation zone. Helicopter installations require
the rotorcraft spectrum with elevated low frequency content. Fixed wing fuselage
installations shall use the jet aircraft spectrum unless engine proximity warrants
the propeller spectrum.

### 7.2 Duration

Each axis shall be subjected to vibration for 60 minutes per axis for a total
exposure of 180 minutes. The spectrum shall be applied sequentially along the three
orthogonal axes corresponding to the equipment installation orientation. Performance
monitoring during the final 5 minutes of each axis shall confirm absence of resonant
amplification.

## 8. SHOCK TESTING

Operational shock pulses of 20 g amplitude with 11 millisecond duration shall be
applied along each of the six principal axes. The pulse shape shall approximate
a half sine waveform within plus or minus 15 percent. Crash safety shock pulses
of 75 g shall be applied in the forward direction only and need not be powered
during application.

## 9. DOCUMENTATION

A consolidated qualification report shall summarize all test results, anomalies,
and corrective actions in accordance with the contract data requirements list.
The report shall be retained for the operational lifetime of the qualified
equipment and made available to the procuring authority upon request.
