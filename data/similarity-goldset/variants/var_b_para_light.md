# Environmental Test Procedure for Aircraft Equipment Qualification

<!-- Page 1 -->

## 1. Purpose Statement

The present document establishes the methodology for environmental qualification
testing applicable to airborne hardware destined for fixed wing and rotary wing
platforms within the defense aviation domain. The procedures defined here set
forth the minimum qualification baseline that suppliers must demonstrate ahead
of delivery acceptance. Any departure from this baseline calls for written
approval from the engineering authority who holds responsibility.

## 2. Tailoring of the Program

Which test sequences must be carried out for any equipment category is specified
through the applicability matrix appearing in Table 2-1. Tailoring decisions are
to factor in the operational profile, mission criticality, along with prior
qualification heritage of the candidate equipment. A tailoring rationale memo
must be submitted by the supplier to the procuring authority no later than 14
days following contract award. Where the platform exposure profile is bounded
by an earlier test, reuse of legacy qualification data is allowed.

## 3. Standards Cited

The publications listed below are invoked within the limits indicated in the
sections that follow. When this document and any cited reference disagree, the
text of this document is governing. Laboratory test methods are established by
MIL-STD-810H. Coverage for commercial transport applications comes from RTCA
DO-160G. Counterfeit avoidance during sample preparation is addressed by SAE
AS6171. Ground vehicle interfaces fall under ISO 16750.

## 4. Conditions for the Laboratory

### 4.1 Climatic Chamber Capability

Testing of temperature, humidity, and altitude requires a chamber that can
maintain setpoints within an envelope of plus or minus 2 degrees Celsius and
5 percent relative humidity. A clearance of at least 150 millimeters around
the test article on every side must be available within the chamber working
volume. Following door opening, the chamber recovery time should not surpass
10 minutes for nominal setpoints.

### 4.2 Vibration Apparatus Capability

Application of mechanical vibration uses an electrodynamic shaker that can
produce the spectra specified in Section 7. Across the test bandwidth, the
fixture must yield a measured transmissibility ratio between 0.9 and 1.1.
Acceleration control is performed using triaxial accelerometers attached
directly to the mounting interface of the test article.

## 5. Cycling of Temperature

### 5.1 Profile Specification

Twelve successive cycles between minus 40 and plus 71 degrees Celsius make up
the thermal profile, with each extreme held for 60 minutes. Unless the equipment
design explicitly tolerates faster excursions, transitions must remain at or
below 5 degrees Celsius per minute. During the last two cycles, power must be
applied to confirm functional performance under thermal stress.

### 5.2 Acceptance Criteria

While powered, the equipment must stay within its specification limits, and
within 5 minutes of returning to ambient conditions, full functionality must
be recovered. Test failure is constituted by any visible damage such as
cracking, warping, or degradation of seals, regardless of functional
performance. Anomalous indications observed during the cycling sequence must
be documented by the supplier.

## 6. Exposure to Humidity

The humidity test runs as 10 successive 24 hour cycles during which the chamber
relative humidity remains above 95 percent and temperatures cycle between 30
and 60 degrees Celsius. Although condensation on the equipment exterior is
allowed, internal moisture ingress causes test failure. Functional checks must
be performed at the end of every cycle and at the close of the exposure
sequence.

## 7. Random Vibration Application

### 7.1 Choice of Spectrum

Selection of the random vibration spectrum is made from the categories given
in Table 7-1 according to the platform installation zone. Installations on
helicopters call for the rotorcraft spectrum with raised low frequency content.
Installations in fixed wing fuselages use the jet aircraft spectrum unless
proximity to engines justifies the propeller spectrum.

### 7.2 Time Duration

Each axis sees vibration for 60 minutes, producing a total exposure of 180
minutes. Application of the spectrum follows the three orthogonal axes that
correspond to the equipment installation orientation. During the final 5
minutes of each axis, performance monitoring confirms that resonant
amplification is absent.

## 8. Shock Test Procedure

Operational shock pulses with 20 g amplitude and 11 millisecond duration are
applied along each of the six principal axes. The pulse shape must approximate
a half sine waveform within plus or minus 15 percent. Forward direction is the
only one in which crash safety shock pulses of 75 g are applied, and these
need not be powered during application.

## 9. Documentation Requirements

In line with the contract data requirements list, a consolidated qualification
report summarizes all test results, anomalies, along with corrective actions.
The report is retained throughout the operational lifetime of the qualified
equipment and made available to the procuring authority on request.
